# My Cybersecurity Journey: Penetration Testing, Client Delivery, and Security Research

Cybersecurity is not just about finding vulnerabilities. It also requires understanding business context, communicating clearly with stakeholders, validating technical risk, and delivering reports that help organizations make improvements. I’m building this portfolio to document my professional experience, technical learning, and research that is appropriate for public disclosure.

I currently work as a **Security Analyst focused on penetration testing**. I have independently handled engagements for **7+ clients**, managing responsibilities across the assessment lifecycle. This has included attending client meetings, understanding assessment requirements, carrying out testing, validating findings, preparing reports, and supporting delivery.

## End-to-end penetration testing

Handling an engagement independently has taught me that technical testing is only one part of the job. The work starts with understanding scope and expectations, continues through systematic testing and evidence validation, and ends with a report that clearly explains risk and remediation.

Client communication matters throughout that process. Meetings help clarify requirements and constraints, while accurate reporting helps stakeholders understand what was found, why it matters, and what needs to change.

My assessment interests span web applications, APIs, infrastructure, authentication and authorization, access control, business logic, session handling, and security misconfigurations. I aim to distinguish confirmed vulnerabilities from scanner observations and to provide reproducible evidence with practical remediation guidance.

## Red teaming and identity security

My security interests also extend to red-team operations and adversary simulation. Areas I’ve worked with or studied include Active Directory enumeration, attack-path analysis, post-exploitation concepts, phishing-based assessments, and hybrid identity security.

Identity misconfigurations can create paths between systems that appear separate—for example, between on-premises infrastructure and cloud identity services. Understanding these relationships helps assess risk beyond individual hosts or endpoints.

## Bug bounty and independent security research

Outside regular assessment work, I pursue independent security research and participate in vulnerability disclosure programs. My research interests include access control, business logic, authentication flows, rate limiting, information exposure, and application misconfiguration.

I have earned **$4,000+ in bug bounty rewards** through independent security research. I share selected milestones on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/madhavan77777/). This article intentionally does not name individual programs. Detailed case studies will be published only when the program’s disclosure rules and any applicable authorization permit it.

Responsible research means respecting scope, minimizing data access, avoiding disruption, and giving affected organizations a clear path to remediation.

## AI application security

I’m also exploring the security of AI-enabled applications. This includes prompt-injection behavior, data exposure risks, error handling, and the way an application’s backend enforces permissions and controls access to information.

A model response by itself does not prove a vulnerability. The important question is whether the behavior creates a demonstrable security impact in the surrounding application.

## Tools and automation

My toolkit includes tools used for web testing, reconnaissance, crawling, and vulnerability assessment, such as Burp Suite, Nmap, Nuclei, sqlmap, ffuf, Katana, and httpx. I’m also interested in building automation that organizes asset discovery and assessment workflows.

Automation can improve consistency and reduce repetitive work, but it cannot replace sound scope management, manual verification, or clear reporting.

## What I’m building next

This GitHub portfolio will collect sanitized write-ups, authorized lab notes, automation projects, and practical lessons. My goal is to show the process behind the result: the question, the testing method, the evidence, the impact, and the remediation.

I believe good security work is not simply finding a weakness. It is proving it responsibly, communicating it accurately, and helping make the system safer.
