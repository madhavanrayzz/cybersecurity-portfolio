# My Cybersecurity Journey: Penetration Testing, Client Delivery, and Security Research

Cybersecurity is not just about finding vulnerabilities. It also requires understanding business context, communicating clearly with stakeholders, validating technical risk, and delivering reports that help organizations make improvements.

I currently work as a **Security Analyst focused on penetration testing**. I have independently handled engagements for **7+ clients**, including client meetings, assessment execution, vulnerability validation, report preparation, and delivery.

## End-to-end penetration testing

The work starts with understanding scope and expectations, continues through systematic testing and evidence validation, and ends with a report that explains risk and remediation. My assessment interests span web applications, APIs, infrastructure, authentication and authorization, access control, business logic, session handling, and security misconfigurations.

## Red teaming and identity security

My interests also extend to red-team operations and adversary simulation, including Active Directory enumeration, attack-path analysis, and hybrid identity security.

## Bug bounty and independent security research

I pursue independent security research and participate in vulnerability disclosure programs. My research interests include access control, business logic, authentication flows, rate limiting, information exposure, and application misconfiguration.

I have earned **$4,000+ in bug bounty rewards** through independent security research. I share selected milestones on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/madhavan77777/). This article intentionally does not name individual programs. Detailed case studies will be published only when disclosure rules and authorization permit it.

Responsible research means respecting scope, minimizing data access, avoiding disruption, and giving affected organizations a clear path to remediation.

## AI application security

I’m exploring the security of AI-enabled applications, including prompt-injection behavior, data exposure risks, error handling, and backend permission enforcement. A model response alone does not prove a vulnerability; the important question is whether the behavior creates demonstrable security impact in the surrounding application.

## Tools and automation

My toolkit includes Burp Suite, Nmap, Nuclei, sqlmap, ffuf, Katana, and httpx. Automation can improve consistency, but it cannot replace scope management, manual verification, or clear reporting.

## What I’m building next

This portfolio will collect sanitized write-ups, authorized lab notes, automation projects, and practical lessons. My goal is to explain the question, testing method, evidence, impact, and remediation.
